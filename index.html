Research

One laser step, three kinds of devices

Laser-induced graphene (LIG) forms directly on a polymer surface. Because it is porous and conducts electricity, the same material can kill microbes, filter water and air, and sense what passes through it.

STEP 1 · POLYMER

Start with a plastic

A polyimide sheet or felt, or a polyethersulfone membrane, sits under a CO₂ laser in ordinary air.

STEP 2 · LASE

Scan the beam

Local heating from the beam converts the surface into a porous graphene foam, following any pattern you draw.

STEP 3 · USE

Conductive, porous, built in

No solvents or masks. The graphene stays on the part, ready to carry current, absorb light or sense.

Antimicrobial & antiviral surfaces

Graphene surfaces that inactivate bacteria and viruses using low voltage, light, or both.

  • Graphene-coated air filters: ~99% bacterial and 100% viral removal at ~0.3 VACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2021
  • Graphene composite adhesive tape: up to 99.99% bacterial killing with light plus currentCarbon 2022
  • LED-activated carbon nanomaterial surfaces: up to 99.1% killing in 20 minutesACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2026
  • LIG-wrapped tri-metal organic framework and silver-doped LIG compositesAdv. Eng. Mater. 2024 · Environ. Sci.: Nano 2024

Membranes & filters

Graphene composite membranes and filter media for water treatment and air purification.

  • Simple, scalable fabrication of LIG composite membranes for water treatmentACS ES&T Water 2021
  • Hydrophilic silica-doped LIG with enhanced anti-adhesion performanceAdv. Mater. Interfaces 2026
  • Dual-function membrane for CO₂ capture and electrocatalytic reductionChemSusChem 2025

About

Dr. Chetan Prakash Sharma

Leads LITE Laboratory as a research initiative.

Chetan is a postdoctoral researcher in Chemical and Materials Engineering at the New Jersey Institute of Technology. His work develops laser-induced graphene materials and devices for antimicrobial surfaces, water-treatment membranes, air filters and electrochemical sensors.

During his PhD at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev he co-developed a low-voltage, virus- and bacteria-killing graphene air filter that was later commercialized. He now works on impedimetric microfluidic sensors and real-time membrane fouling monitoring.
